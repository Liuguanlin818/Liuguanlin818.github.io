    Research Interests

    Artificial intelligence for health: from non-invasive brain signals to clinical records

    I am interested in learning clinically useful structure from messy, high-dimensional biomedical data, and I work on two modalities. The first is non-invasive brain activity, specifically the decoding side of brain–computer interfaces: EEG decoders have to survive the non-stationarity of neural signals, whose statistics drift across sessions and differ widely between people, while staying cheap enough to run on a wearable headset. That calls for architectures that respect the modality — convolutional and graph models for the spatial and temporal dependencies between channels, spiking networks for event-driven, energy-efficient inference on neuromorphic hardware, and neuro-fuzzy components wherever a decision has to remain inspectable. Applications I care about include affective state recognition, seizure detection and auditory attention decoding. The second modality is the electronic health record: longitudinal, irregular, and full of relations that are never written down. There I use dynamic and heterogeneous graph representation learning for medication recommendation under drug–drug interaction constraints, and for outcomes such as mortality and readmission, treating interpretability as a requirement of the model rather than an afterthought.

    Large language models and agentic AI

    The second line is the language side. I am interested in LLMs both as generators and as agents: systems that plan, call tools, carry memory and revise their own output over a long horizon, instead of answering a single prompt. The work I have contributed to in this direction is on evidence-aware generation, where a detector returns a calibrated uncertainty rather than a bare label — the same demand that clinical decision support places on a model. I want to keep working on retrieval-augmented and tool-using agents, on multi-agent orchestration for tasks whose intermediate steps can be checked, and on the reliability questions that follow: hallucination, long-horizon drift, robustness under distribution shift, and evaluation that is honest about all three. Misinformation and rumour detection is the application I know best, but I am just as interested in what changes when the corpus is a patient record and an unsupported claim is expensive.
