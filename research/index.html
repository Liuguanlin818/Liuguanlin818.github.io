    Research

    My research moves along one line: when the relations that matter are not written down anywhere, learn them from the data and let them evolve over time. I first developed this idea on electronic health records, where the interactions between diagnoses, procedures and medications are implicit; I now want to apply it to EEG, where there is no predefined map from electrodes to brain regions.

    From EHR to EEG: the same problem twice

    Shared difficultyPrevious work — EHRThis plan — EEG / BCI
    No explicit structure in the data Interactions among diagnoses, procedures and medicines are implicit in the record, so a static graph cannot represent them. Neuro-mechanisms are complex and EEG has no predefined structure linking electrodes to brain regions.
    Spatial and temporal dependencies at once The structural correlation among heterogeneous medical events and the sequential dependency across a patient’s visits must be modelled together. The spatial correlation across EEG channels and the temporal dependency of the evolving brain network must be modelled together.
    The same remedy Learn the structure from the data instead of assuming it, and let it change over time as a dynamic graph.

    Research Directions

    1. Generalisable and calibration-efficient EEG decoding

    I aim to build EEG decoders that transfer across subjects with minimal per-user calibration. I will represent brain connectivity as a dynamic graph and learn subject-invariant yet individually adaptive representations with contrastive objectives.

    2. Self-supervised brain foundation models for wearable, low-density EEG

    I want to pretrain a compact EEG foundation model on unlabelled recordings from dry-sensor, low-density headsets, combining masked and autoregressive prediction in the time–frequency domain with prototype-based contrastive learning, so that oscillatory rhythms and cross-channel dependencies are captured without manual annotation.

    3. Interpretable and closed-loop BCI for cognitive-state monitoring

    I want decoders whose decisions can be explained in terms of brain regions, frequency bands and connectivity patterns, and to embed them in a closed loop that adapts as cognitive state drifts over long real-world sessions. Target states: attention, mental workload, fatigue and mood, with mood and depression monitoring as the clinical-facing application.

    Current Proposal: Adaptive Brain-Graph Learning for Cross-Subject EEG Decoding and Mood Monitoring

    Background

    Depression is a pervasive mental disorder, and early recognition and timely intervention can considerably alleviate the suffering of patients. Compared with physician reviews and psychiatric questionnaires, electroencephalography (EEG) is an objective physiological signal that is not influenced by human consciousness and cannot easily be imitated, which makes EEG-based depression recognition attractive. The same structural problem appeared in my EHR work, where the relations among medical events were also implicit and which I solved with a weighted dynamic network.

    Challenges

    • The complexity of the neuro-mechanisms means that EEG data lacks an explicit topological structure.
    • Capturing spatio-temporal features is challenging from multi-channel, high-density EEG signals.

    Methodology

    1. Explore the brain-network connectivity state reflected by EEG and compute the adjacency matrix.
    2. Use a multi-layer Temporal Graph Neural Network (TGNN) to fuse multi-dimensional information, modelling the spatial correlation between channels and the temporal dependency of brain-network changes.
    3. Compute node importance from the graph topology and retain the most representative nodes for the depression prediction.

    Expected contributions

    A method for depression detection from EEG that adaptively constructs a plausible brain-network structure while preserving its dynamic changes, validated by extensive experiments on publicly available datasets. In the longer term, I want to develop a general model that integrates heterogeneous sources such as ECG, EOG and GSR towards a neurocognitive brain–machine interface.
