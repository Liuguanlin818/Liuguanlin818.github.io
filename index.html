    About Me

    I am a computer scientist at Shandong Normal University, where I work with Prof. Xiaomei Yu and Assoc. Prof. Hongchen Wu. My work sits where machine learning meets medicine: I build dynamic-graph and multi-view representation models that turn irregular electronic health records into safe medication recommendations, and I study large language models for detecting misinformation in online social networks.

    I am applying for MPhil and PhD positions starting in 2027. My aim is to carry the same idea — learn the structure from the data instead of assuming it — from clinical records to the brain: EEG decoders that transfer across subjects with little or no per-user calibration, and whose decisions can be read in terms of brain regions, frequency bands and connectivity patterns.

    Research Interests

    Generalisable and calibration-efficient EEG decoding

    Decoders that transfer across subjects with minimal per-user calibration. I want to represent brain connectivity as a dynamic graph and learn subject-invariant yet individually adaptive representations with contrastive objectives, bringing the structure-discovery idea from my dynamic heterogeneous network work on EHR data into EEG.

    Self-supervised brain foundation models for wearable, low-density EEG

    Pretraining a compact EEG foundation model on unlabelled recordings from dry-sensor, low-density headsets, combining masked and autoregressive prediction in the time–frequency domain with prototype-based contrastive learning, so that oscillatory rhythms and cross-channel dependencies are captured without manual annotation.

    Interpretable and closed-loop BCI for cognitive-state monitoring

    Decoders whose decisions can be explained in terms of brain regions, frequency bands and connectivity patterns, embedded in a closed loop that adapts as cognitive state drifts over long real-world sessions. Target states: attention, mental workload, fatigue and mood, with mood and depression monitoring as the clinical-facing application.
